# Chapter Outlining

Long fics die in the "muddy middle" without a map. An outline keeps the arc
shaped and each chapter pulling its weight — while staying loose enough to follow
inspiration.

## Shape the overall arc first

Pin the big beats before chapter detail:
- **Hook / inciting incident** — what pulls the reader and the protagonist in.
- **Rising complications** — escalating obstacles; each raises stakes or cost.
- **Midpoint** — a reversal or revelation that changes the game (great anti-sag
  tool for long works).
- **Crisis / low point** — the protagonist's worst moment; the relationship or
  goal looks lost.
- **Climax** — the confrontation everything has built toward.
- **Resolution** — fallout and the new normal; pay off the emotional promise.

For slow burns and relationship fics, also map the **relationship beats**
(meeting → tension → near-miss → setback → commitment) across the same arc.

## Then outline chapters

For each chapter, jot:
1. **Goal** — what this chapter accomplishes for plot AND character/relationship.
2. **POV & setting** — whose eyes, where, when.
3. **Beats** — 2–5 bullet events.
4. **Turn** — how the situation is different at the end vs. the start. A chapter
   with no change is a candidate for cutting or merging.
5. **Hook** — the line/question/threat that makes the reader open the next chapter.

## Principles

- **Every chapter turns.** Movement in plot, relationship, or understanding.
- **End on a hook.** Especially for serialized posting — cliffhangers, questions,
  reversals keep subscribers.
- **Outline in pencil.** Treat it as a hypothesis. If the characters pull the
  story somewhere better, update the outline (and the story bible) and keep going.
- **Track word budget loosely.** If chapter 4 of a planned 20 is already at the
  midpoint beat, your arc is front-loaded — rebalance.
