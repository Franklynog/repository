# Character Voice

In fanfiction, characterization is the product. Readers come for *these* people;
"OOC" is the most common complaint. Each character should be recognizable from
their lines alone, even with the dialogue tags removed.

## Build a voice profile (per major character)

- **Diction** — formal vs. casual, slang, profanity level, vocabulary range,
  verbal tics and catchphrases.
- **Rhythm** — terse vs. rambling; complete sentences vs. fragments; how they
  handle silence.
- **Emotional tells** — what they do under stress, joy, fear; what they hide and
  how the hiding shows.
- **Values & blind spots** — what they'd never say or do; what they rationalize.
- **Reference lines** — quote 2–3 canon lines that capture them; write new
  dialogue that could sit beside those quotes.

## In-character vs. growth

Staying in character ≠ freezing the character. People change — but earn it.
- Change should be **gradual** and **motivated** by story events.
- The core stays; the expression shifts. A guarded character opening up still
  opens up *guardedly* at first.
- If a line feels OOC, ask: would canon-them do this *given everything that has
  happened in this story*? If yes, make sure the prose shows the steps that got
  them there.

## Techniques

- **Voice-swap test:** strip dialogue tags from a scene. Can you still tell who's
  speaking? If not, sharpen the distinct voices.
- **Avoid voice bleed:** when you're deep in one POV, characters can start
  sounding like the narrator (or like you). Re-check against the voice profile.
- **Subtext over statement:** strong characters rarely say exactly what they feel.
  Let want and fear show through deflection, action, and what goes unsaid.

## Principle

Plot can be forgiven; flat or OOC characters can't. Protect voice first.
