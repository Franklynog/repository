# Continuity Tracking

Over tens of thousands of words, small contradictions accumulate and break reader
trust. Continuity tracking is the deliberate check that the new material agrees
with everything already established.

## What to check (continuity passes)

1. **Facts** — names, ages, appearances, possessions, locations. Cross-reference
   against the story bible; if it's not in the bible and it'll recur, add it.
2. **Timeline** — does the sequence and elapsed time add up? Day/night, seasons,
   travel time, "three days later" vs. earlier statements.
3. **Knowledge state** — *who knows what, when.* The most common fanfic plot hole:
   a character acting on information they haven't learned yet (or forgetting
   something they were told). Track reveals explicitly.
4. **World rules** — does the new scene obey the established magic/tech/social
   rules, or did a rule bend for convenience? If it must bend, justify it on-page.
5. **Emotional continuity** — do relationships and moods carry over? A fight last
   chapter shouldn't vanish without acknowledgment.
6. **Threads & promises** — are planted foreshadowing and Chekhov's guns still on
   track to pay off? Did this chapter open new threads to log?

## Workflow

- **Before posting a chapter:** run the passes above against the bible and the
  previous 1–2 chapters.
- **When something conflicts:** prefer fixing the *new* text. If canon (your
  story's earlier published chapters) is wrong and can't be retconned quietly,
  acknowledge it in-story rather than ignoring it.
- **Log fixes:** update the story bible so the corrected fact becomes the new
  reference.

## Principle

Readers forgive a slow chapter; they don't forgive being lied to about the world.
Consistency is how a long fic earns trust.
