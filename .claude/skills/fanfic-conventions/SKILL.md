# Fanfic Conventions

Fanfiction lives in a community with strong norms. Getting the metadata and
conventions right is how the right readers find your fic — and how you keep their
trust by setting accurate expectations.

## Posting metadata

- **Rating.** Match content honestly (G / Teen / Mature / Explicit, per platform).
  Mis-rating is the fastest way to lose readers and get reported.
- **Archive warnings.** Apply the standard ones where applicable (graphic
  violence, major character death, non-con, underage) or mark "no archive warnings
  apply" / "choose not to use" deliberately, not by accident.
- **Tags.** Tag for ships (use the community's name order/format), characters,
  genres, and tropes. Tag the experience you're delivering. Don't "tag-spam"
  ships/characters that barely appear — it annoys readers filtering for them.
- **Content/trigger warnings.** Beyond archive warnings, flag sensitive content
  (self-harm, eating disorders, abuse) in the notes so readers can choose. Err
  toward warning.
- **Summary.** A short, hooky blurb that conveys tone and premise without
  spoiling. This sells the click more than anything else.
- **Author's notes.** Keep them brief; put long warnings/context at the top,
  chatter at the bottom. Don't bury the chapter under a wall of A/N.

## Craft conventions

- **POV & tense:** pick and hold one (e.g. third-limited past, or first present)
  unless deliberately alternating. Mark POV switches clearly (scene breaks,
  headers). Head-hopping reads as a mistake.
- **Trope literacy:** deliver on tagged tropes. A "slow burn" must actually burn
  slowly with sustained tension; "enemies to lovers" needs real antagonism before
  the turn; "fix-it" should fix the thing readers came for.
- **Canon stance:** signal canon-compliant vs. AU vs. canon-divergent, and from
  what point. Readers want to know the rules of your sandbox.

## Serialized posting

- **Consistent chapter length & cadence** builds subscriber trust; under-promise
  and over-deliver on schedule.
- **End chapters on hooks** to retain subscribers between updates.
- **Respond to comments** where you can — community engagement compounds.
- **Don't post unfinished WIPs you may abandon** without marking them WIP.

## Principle

Accurate, generous metadata is a contract with the reader: it brings the people
who'll love the fic and warns away those who won't. Honor it.
