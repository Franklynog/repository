# Prose Craft

A strong outline still needs strong sentences. This is the line-level craft that
makes a scene immersive instead of a summary.

## Core techniques

- **Show, then tell (sparingly).** Dramatize emotion through action, body, and
  choice rather than naming it. "Her hands wouldn't stop shaking" beats "she was
  nervous." Use direct telling only to pace through low-stakes connective tissue.
- **Ground in the senses.** Anchor scenes with concrete, specific detail — and not
  just sight. Sound, smell, temperature, and texture make a place real. One sharp
  specific detail beats three generic ones.
- **Filter words.** Trim "she saw / he felt / I noticed" — they put a pane of
  glass between reader and experience. "The door creaked open" > "she heard the
  door creak open."
- **Dialogue.** Keep it doing work: revealing character, advancing conflict, or
  both. Cut throat-clearing ("Well, um, I think that..."). Let beats and action
  carry emotion instead of stacking adverb-laden tags; "said" is usually enough.
- **Vary rhythm.** Mix sentence lengths. Short sentences hit hard and fast — use
  them for impact and action. Longer ones breathe and reflect. Monotone rhythm
  reads flat regardless of content.

## Common fixes

| Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| "Feels flat / distant" | Cut filter words; deepen POV; add a sensory anchor. |
| "Too telly" | Convert stated emotions into shown action/body language. |
| "Rushed" | Slow the important beat down; add interiority and reaction. |
| "Purple / overwritten" | Cut adjective/adverb pileups; keep the one best image. |
| "Dialogue is stiff" | Read it aloud; add interruptions, subtext, contractions. |

## Principle

Write the scene the reader experiences moment-to-moment, not a report of what
happened. Then cut every word that doesn't earn its place.
