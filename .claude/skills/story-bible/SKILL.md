# Story Bible

The #1 failure mode of long fanfiction is contradicting yourself: eye colors that
change, a character in two places at once, a magic rule broken three chapters
later. A story bible is the reference you check against to prevent that.

## Keep a living bible file

For any work expected to run long, maintain a document (e.g. `STORY_BIBLE.md` in
the project, or a pinned note) with these sections. Update it the moment a new
fact is established in the prose.

1. **Premise & tags** — one-paragraph logline; ship(s); rating; key tropes;
   content warnings; canon divergence point ("AU from S3E5").
2. **Characters** — for each: canon vs. your interpretation, age, appearance,
   speech patterns, motivations, secrets, arc. Include OCs in full.
3. **Relationships** — who knows whom, current status of each dynamic, and how it
   should evolve. Note what each character knows vs. what the reader knows.
4. **World rules** — magic/tech/politics systems and their hard limits. Write the
   limits down; readers notice when rules bend for plot convenience.
5. **Timeline** — chronology of events, including backstory and off-page events.
   Track in-story time elapsed (seasons, ages, deadlines).
6. **Locations** — recurring settings and their fixed details.
7. **Threads & promises** — open mysteries, foreshadowing planted, Chekhov's guns
   awaiting payoff. Mark each as planted / developing / paid off.

## Workflow

- **Before drafting a chapter:** skim the bible for relevant characters, rules,
  and open threads.
- **While drafting:** when you invent a new fact (a name, a rule, a backstory
  beat), add it to the bible immediately.
- **After drafting:** record any new threads or promises you planted.

## Principle

If a detail will need to stay consistent later, it belongs in the bible. Memory
fails over 100k words; the document doesn't.
